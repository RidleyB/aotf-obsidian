
(see also [[Fable of the Three Great Beasts|fable]])

In the early days of Mordt, Imar, Nimyr, and Thagar waged war for the lives of the mortals that dwelled there. 

Each of the three were lesser celestial beings; not gods, but powerful enough to assume the role with enough time and worship from mortal races. In binding their mortal lives in the tenets and ideologies of a Celestial, their soul would empower that Celestial, growing their power. Without the help of the mortals dwelling there, Imar, Nimyr, and Thagar would starve, falling into darkness and obscurity, lost to the endless void.

[[Imar]] is the only Celestial among the three to have been defeated. Nimyr's devilry and blinding radiance is what pierced the thick hide of darkness and shadow of Imar, ultimately killing him as the world knew him. Still, a Celestial can still affect the mortal world long after their death; Imar cursed the lands of Mordt, poisoning the chances of survival for Nimyr or Thagar by killing the remaining mortals living there.

Imar's curse came in the way of an unrelenting shadow, seeping disease and sickness from the earth, and a twisting of Mortal races into soulless, mindless beasts. Following the war, the mortal populations dwindled nearly to the point of oblivion.

[[Thagar]], or Thagar the Hungering was also defeated by Nimyr, though the nature of his covenant allowed him to live on through the binding pacts he made with mortals. On the Isle of Sunder he waits, dormant, growing in power as Vampirism spreads throughout the land. His eldest vampires say that sometime soon he will wake, and bring about a new era for Mordt; a red dawn.

Vampirism, or the Hungering is the mark that Thagar left upon mortal lives; entire settlements overtaken by vampires, only to turn feral for lack of food. Wolf among sheep, out for blood. Nimyr has villainized vampires in the hopes that culling enough will keep Thagar from ever rising again.

[[Nimyr]] is the victor of this great war, for better or worse. Having felled Imar and Thagar, Nimyr's radiance stood victorious; but he needed to act quickly. In order to save the mortals he waged war over, he approached them as a savior. He offered them the gift of light in a world of darkness; warmth, radiance, fertile land and the promise of eternal love. 

Some accepted this poisoned apple, though some come to understand on some level Nimyr's devilry; those that accepted his gift became the first Nimyrians, or later established as Ascendant Tieflings. These Tieflings established the Inner provinces of Nimyria, and began construction of the Dawnspire.

Those that opposed Nimyr were met with a different kind of radiance; He waged war upon them , while letting Imar's curse overtake them. Many were corrupted by Imar, others were "cleansed" from the surface of Mordt, leaving only dust and melted stone monuments.

Still, Humans persisted as the centuries went on. Enslaved and treated as second-class, though tolerance grew over time in the outer provinces. Over time, a second class of Tieflings formed also- one with less innate power as the Ascendants that came before. Already, the promises of Nimyr had begun to fade. Whether by mixture of Tiefling and Human lineage, or the overall weakening of Nimyr's power over time, this lower-tier of Tiefling would quickly become the predominant race. Through centuries of propaganda and burying the truth, people forgot the War of the Great Beasts; Imar and Thagar relegated to shadow, while the rest of the world stands in blinding light.
